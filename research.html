Research

Understanding the injured brain.

From mechanism to intervention. We use advanced neuroimaging and computational methods to study how the brain changes after traumatic injury — and how it can recover.

Featured Project · DOD Award

Transcranial Photobiomodulation (tPBM) for Chronic TBI

Traumatic brain injury causes microvascular damage that reduces cerebral blood flow and accelerates neurodegeneration, a process that is especially severe in older adults and contributes to long-term cognitive decline. There is currently no widely effective treatment.

The Kim Lab is leading a four-year Phase II randomized double-blind clinical trial of transcranial photobiomodulation — a non-invasive therapy that delivers near-infrared light across the forehead to stimulate cerebral blood flow and brain function. Eighty participants aged 55 and older with chronic TBI will receive either active or sham treatment over six weeks, paired with neuropsychological testing and MRI imaging at the CUNY Advanced Science Research Center and NYU Rusk Rehabilitation.

"For older adults with TBI, the key is to develop an easy-to-use and well-tolerated treatment with minimal side effects. Our goal is to evaluate whether tPBM can enhance brain function and ultimately reduce TBI-induced neurodegeneration." Dr. Junghoon Kim · Principal Investigator

Co-Investigators: Dr. Tamara Bushnik (NYU Grossman School of Medicine), Dr. Dan Iosifescu (NYU Grossman School of Medicine), Dr. Muhammad Abubakar Yamin (CUNY School of Medicine), Dr. Jacek Dmochowski (The City College of New York).

Other research areas

More from the lab.

Each thread feeds the others — basic science discoveries inform the trials, and clinical observations sharpen the basic science.

Vascular biology

Cerebrovascular Reactivity in Moderate-to-Severe TBI

We use resting-state perfusion MRI — including arterial spin labeling and BOLD-based CVR mapping — to characterize how the brain regulates its own blood flow after injury. Longitudinal studies trace how cerebral blood flow shifts across the first year of recovery, revealing patterns of hyper- and hypoperfusion that correlate with cognitive outcomes.

Anchor papers: Gaggi et al. 2023 Neuroimage Clin; Xu et al. 2021 J Neurotrauma; Ware et al. 2020 J Neurotrauma.

Imaging biomarkers

Neuroimaging Biomarkers of TBI Recovery

By combining structural MRI, diffusion tensor imaging, arterial spin labeling, and dynamic contrast-enhanced MRI, we identify imaging signatures that predict who will recover — and how. Our work has characterized blood-brain-barrier dysfunction, axonal injury variability, and connectomic disruption across the moderate-to-severe TBI spectrum.

Anchor papers: Ware et al. 2022 Neuroimage Clin; Osmanlıoğlu et al. 2022 Hum Brain Mapp; Choi et al. 2019 Neuroimage Clin.

Outcomes & recovery

Cognitive Rehabilitation Outcomes

How do patients with moderate-to-severe TBI recover cognitively across years — and what predicts that trajectory? We combine standardized neuropsychological testing with diffuse axonal injury imaging to map the heterogeneity of recovery and identify patient characteristics that shape it.

Anchor papers: Rabinowitz et al. 2018 JINS; Hart et al. 2019 Neuropsychol Rehabil; Kim et al. 2014 JINS.
