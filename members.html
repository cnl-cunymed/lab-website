Lab Members

The people behind the work.

A close-knit team of clinical neuroimaging researchers — bridging neurology, neuroscience, biomedical engineering, and computational methods.

Principal Investigator

Junghoon Kim, PhD

Principal Investigator · Assistant Medical Professor

Dr. Kim leads the Clinical Neuroimaging Lab at the CUNY School of Medicine, where his work combines advanced neuroimaging and neuropsychological methods to study neurodegeneration and neuroplasticity after traumatic brain injury. His research focuses on the mechanisms of functional recovery, the effects of clinical interventions such as neuromodulation, psychoactive drugs, and cognitive training, and the development of novel neuroimaging biomarkers of TBI.

Dr. Kim is the Principal Investigator on a Department of Defense grant supporting a Phase II clinical trial of transcranial photobiomodulation (tPBM) in older adults with chronic TBI. The trial is conducted in collaboration with NYU Rusk Rehabilitation, the CUNY Advanced Science Research Center, and The City College of New York.

Postdoctoral Researcher

Muhammad Abubakar Yamin, PhD

Research Scientist

Dr. Yamin's research focuses on neuroimaging analysis, brain functional connectivity, and the application of machine learning to medical imaging — particularly for disease classification in neurological conditions. He serves as Co-Investigator on the lab's DOD-funded tPBM clinical trial, where he leads the computational analysis of MRI biomarkers tied to treatment response.

PhD Student

Yifei Li

First-Year PhD Student

Yifei is a first-year PhD student in the CUNY Graduate Center Neuroscience program. In the Kim Lab, she works on traumatic brain injury, using deep learning on clinical neuroimaging data. The pull, for her, is the gap between biomarker discovery and clinical use, and what it would take to close it.

She joined CNL after three years in Christoph Anacker's lab at Columbia, where her research focused on stress vulnerability and resilience in rodent models. That work moved across circuit imaging, behavioral neuroscience, and serotonin signaling. She also holds an MA in Biotechnology from Columbia.

James Barefoot, BA

Master's Student · Cognitive Neuroscience

James is a master's student in Cognitive Neuroscience at the CUNY Graduate Center, completing pre-medical coursework as a post-baccalaureate student alongside his degree in preparation for medical school.

His thesis tests how reliably brain entropy can be measured from functional MRI (fMRI) across repeat scans, then compares healthy controls with moderate-to-severe traumatic brain injury (TBI) patients, both at a single time point and across recovery. He is also co-authoring a scoping review on endocrine dysregulation after TBI and the therapeutic potential of transcranial photobiomodulation (tPBM).

Elias Koukoulis

Master's Student · Behavioral Neuroscience

Elias is a master's student in the Behavioral Neuroscience program at Queens College. In the lab he develops individual-specific analysis methods, working on one of the central difficulties in traumatic brain injury (TBI) research: patients vary so widely that group averages can obscure what is happening to any one of them.

He works with a longitudinal dataset of cerebral blood flow measurements, generating control-referenced voxel-wise z-score maps that compare each patient against a healthy control group point by point through the brain, quantifying abnormal perfusion one person at a time. His research examines how these individualized perfusion measures track with neuropsychological outcomes across recovery, with the goal of better understanding recovery trajectories after TBI.

Vaidehi Patel, MS

Research Collaborator · Cognitive Neuroscience

Vaidehi studies neurovascular coupling in traumatic brain injury patients at the Clinical Neuroimaging Lab. She holds an MS in Cognitive Neuroscience from the CUNY Graduate Center and a BE in Computer Engineering from the University of Mumbai.

She continues to collaborate with the lab on publishing this research.
